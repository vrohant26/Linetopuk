Overview

As part of the Nature Networks 3 (NN3) initiative, a visitor counter was installed along the permissive path at Slade Farm to support evaluation and monitoring efforts. Delivered in partnership with Fieldwork Studio CIC, the project aimed to strengthen connectivity between protected sites through improved public access, community engagement, and biodiversity enhancement

Installation & Setup

The installation process was smooth and professionally managed. The team maintained clear communication and worked flexibly to minimise disruption to farm operations and visitors. The counter has operated reliably since commissioning, and the accompanying data EcoPC app has proven intuitive and accessible. Linetop provided helpful setup guidance, ensuring the system was quickly integrated into the site’s monitoring framework.

Funding & Purpose

The installation was funded through the Welsh Government’s Nature Networks Fund (Round 3). The visitor counter plays a key role in evidencing the social and ecological benefits of improved access to nature, aligning with the fund’s broader goals of connectivity and conservation

Data Use & Impact

Visitor data collected from the counter is being used to:

  • Inform habitat management and infrastructure planning, balancing footfall with habitat sensitivity.
  • Support future funding applications with robust evidence of public engagement.
  • Contribute to impact reporting for funders, showcasing the value of investment in access improvements.
  • Enable regional collaboration by sharing anonymised data with local partners to build a broader picture of nature-connected visitor behaviour.

Conclusion

The Slade Farm visitor counter has become an integral tool for monitoring and planning within the NN3 project. Its successful deployment demonstrates how technology can enhance conservation efforts and support strategic decision-making across protected landscape
